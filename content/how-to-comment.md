---
title: How to Contribute and Comment
---

There are two ways to help with this edition. They differ in **who sees what you
write**, and in **which account you need**. Both are in the **Contribute** menu at
the top of every page.

|                    | Who sees it                                                                                                                    | Account                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Edit on GitHub** | The edition's maintainers review it. The proposal is public on the edition's GitHub repository and shows your GitHub username. | A free [GitHub account](https://github.com/signup)       |
| **Public comment** | Anyone on the internet, with your Hypothes.is username.                                                                        | A free [Hypothes.is account](https://hypothes.is/signup) |

---

## Edit on GitHub

Change the wording on GitHub. Choose **Contribute → Edit on GitHub ↗**: the
page's file opens on GitHub, where you can edit it and propose your change. It goes
to the edition's maintainers as a proposal, and nothing in the edition changes
until they accept it. The proposal is public on the edition's GitHub repository and shows your GitHub
username.

## Public comment

Write in the margin with **Hypothes.is**: highlight a passage and comment on it,
ask a question, or reply to someone else's comment.

### Reading the comments: nothing needed

Anyone can read them, with no account. Choose **Annotate** at the top of the page
(it shows how many comments the page has). A sidebar opens along the right-hand
edge, listing every comment on the page; click one and the passage it belongs to
lights up. Highlighted passages in the text are places someone has said something.

### Leaving your own

1. Sign up at **https://hypothes.is/signup**. It's free and asks for an email
   address and a username.
2. Open the sidebar (**Annotate**) and **Log in** at its top.
3. **Select the text** you want to comment on. A small **Annotate** button
   appears next to it; click it, type, and **Post**.

**Your username appears next to every comment you leave, and anyone can see it.**
Plenty of people use something that isn't their full name. You can edit or delete
your own comments (the **⋮** menu on each) but nobody else's. **Reply** works the
same way, from the sidebar.

### Two things people don't expect

**Comments are public.** They aren't private to your class or your tutor: anyone
on the internet can read them. There is one margin, and everybody shares it.
Write what you'd be happy to have your name on.

> [!warning] Don't put personal information in a comment
> Not your student number, your email address, anything about your health or your
> marks, or anything about anyone else. Once it's posted it's on the open web.

**Last year's comments are still there.** The margin isn't wiped between cohorts.
A good question from two years ago is still a good question; check the date under
the username if you're unsure whether a conversation is live.

### Tags (optional)

Two words help whoever looks after the edition sort comments: **`copy-edit`** (a
mistake: a typo, a broken link, a wrong number) and **`discussion`** (a question
or an idea). While the sidebar is open, a small panel beside it has both as
buttons. Clicking one **copies the word**; paste it into the **Tags** field under
your comment. It can't fill the field in for you: the comment box is in a frame
the page isn't allowed to write into. Most comments have no tag, and that's fine.

### Turning comments off

**Appearance (Aa) → Public annotations: Off** hides the margin on every page of
the edition in this browser. Turn it back on there, or choose **Annotate**.

## If something doesn't work

**Annotate does nothing, or says Hypothes.is didn't load.** Usually a browser
extension (an ad-blocker or privacy blocker) or a network that blocks it. Try a
different browser, or your phone off wi-fi.

**You selected text and no Annotate button appeared.** Select a little more: a
very short selection can miss, and selections across an image or a table
sometimes don't take.

**Your comment vanished after the page was edited.** It hasn't been deleted. When
the words it was attached to change a lot, it moves to the **Orphans** tab at the
top of the sidebar, still there and still yours.

If none of that helps, tell your course coordinator: it's almost always something
at the site's end.
